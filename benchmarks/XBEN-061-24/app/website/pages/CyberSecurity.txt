    <h1>Cyber Security Fundamentals</h1>
    <p>
        Cybersecurity is the practice of protecting systems, networks, and programs from digital attacks. 
        These cyberattacks are usually aimed at accessing, changing, or destroying sensitive information, 
        extorting money from users, or interrupting normal business processes.
    </p>
    
    <h2>Key Concepts</h2>
    <h3>Confidentiality</h3>
    <p>Ensuring that information is accessible only to those authorized to have access. This is typically achieved through encryption, access controls, and authentication mechanisms.</p>
    
    <h3>Integrity</h3>
    <p>Maintaining and assuring the accuracy and completeness of data over its entire lifecycle. This means data cannot be modified in an unauthorized or undetected manner.</p>
    
    <h3>Availability</h3>
    <p>Information must be available when needed. This involves maintaining hardware, performing software upgrades, and ensuring adequate bandwidth.</p>
    
    <h2>Common Threats</h2>
    <ul>
        <li><strong>Phishing</strong> - Social engineering attacks that trick users into revealing sensitive information</li>
        <li><strong>Ransomware</strong> - Malware that encrypts files and demands payment for decryption</li>
        <li><strong>Man-in-the-Middle</strong> - Attacks where communication between two parties is intercepted</li>
        <li><strong>SQL Injection</strong> - Exploiting vulnerabilities in database-driven applications</li>
        <li><strong>Cross-Site Scripting</strong> - Injecting malicious scripts into web pages viewed by others</li>
    </ul>
    
    <h2>Best Practices</h2>
    <p>Organizations should implement a defense-in-depth strategy that includes regular security audits, employee training, and incident response planning.</p>
